# What to confirm with a campground

A directory listing rarely answers these. Choose the ones that matter for the user's rig and plans.

## Will the rig fit

- maximum rig length for the site, and whether that counts the tow vehicle
- pull through or back in
- width for slide rooms
- any low branches, tight turns or weight limits on the way in
- whether a towed car or a second vehicle can park at the site

## Hookups

- electric: 30 amp, 50 amp, or none. The user should know which the rig uses and whether they carry an adapter
- water at the site
- sewer at the site, or a dump station on the grounds
- whether generators are allowed and during which hours, for sites without electric

## Arrival and stay

- availability for the nights needed
- the latest check in time and how after hours arrival works
- the nightly rate and what it includes
- minimum or maximum length of stay
- whether reservations are required or sites are first come

## Rules that catch people out

- age or type restrictions on rigs
- pets
- quiet hours
- whether repairs or maintenance may be done on site, which matters when a mobile technician is coming

## If the rig is disabled

- whether a tow truck can deliver the rig to a site
- whether a mobile RV technician is allowed to work there
- whether the stay can be extended if the repair runs long

## For the assistant

- Report only what a record states. Everything else is a question for the campground.
- Offer two or three questions, not the whole list.
- Use the user's own rig length and electrical needs. If they do not know them, say where to find them: length in the owner's documents, amperage on the power cord plug or the electrical panel.
