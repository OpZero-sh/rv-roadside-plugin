# Identifying an RV component

What to record so a store, a shop or a maker can identify a part without guessing. For where each plate is located, use the connector's `rv_guide` with `section: "plates_and_parts"`. Its guidance and the maker's documentation take precedence over this file.

## Three makers, three sets of numbers

| What | Who made it | Identifier to record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The RV as a whole | The RV maker | Year, make, model and floor plan, plus the VIN |
| A motorhome's chassis | The chassis maker, often a different company | Chassis make and model year, engine, and the VIN |
| An appliance or component | Its own maker | Brand, model number and serial number from the component's data plate |

A part for an appliance is ordered by the appliance's model and serial number, not by the RV's model. A part for the drivetrain is ordered by the chassis details. Only house parts, such as trim, windows and cabinetry, are ordered through the RV maker.

## What to copy from a data plate

- the maker's name
- the model number, complete, with every letter, digit and dash
- the serial number
- any ratings printed on it that a store might ask about: voltage, BTU, capacity, fuel type
- the date of manufacture, if shown

A clear photo of the whole plate is the easiest way to keep all of this. Model numbers often differ by a single suffix that changes which parts fit.

## Labels on the RV itself

- **VIN.** A 17 character vehicle identification number. It is on the RV's identification label and on the registration and insurance documents.
- **Weight label.** Lists the gross vehicle weight rating (GVWR) and axle ratings. Tow companies and tire shops ask for these.
- **Tire information.** The tire and loading label gives the original tire size and inflation pressure. The tire sidewall gives the size and load range actually fitted. If the two differ, tell the shop both.

## Tires

Copy from the sidewall:

- the full size, for example a string beginning with ST or LT followed by width, aspect ratio and rim size
- the load range letter or load index
- the DOT code. Its last four digits are the week and year the tire was made

Tire age matters on RVs because tires often age out before the tread wears. Give the shop the date code and let them advise.

## When the plate is missing or unreadable

- Look for the owner's packet that came with the RV. It usually has a manual for each appliance with the model on the cover.
- The RV maker can often list the components installed, given the VIN.
- Take photos of the component from several sides. A parts counter can often identify it by sight.
- Do not guess a model number. A wrong number wastes a trip and can produce a part that does not fit.

## For the assistant

- Ask for one identifier at a time, and say why the store needs it.
- Repeat numbers back exactly as the user gave them.
- Never state that a part fits. Fit comes from the maker's parts list.
