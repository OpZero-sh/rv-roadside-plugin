# Safety first

Short actions to give before any search. Say the one that applies, plainly, and let the user act. These are general safety practices. Emergency dispatchers and the maker's manual override them.

## When to send someone to 911 first

- fire or smoke from any part of the rig or tow vehicle
- the smell of propane, or a propane alarm
- a carbon monoxide alarm, or people feeling unwell inside the rig with fuel burning appliances or a generator running
- anyone injured or having a medical problem
- the rig stopped where traffic could hit it
- a runaway risk: brakes not holding, the rig on a slope without wheel chocks

Outside the US, use the local emergency number.

## What to say

**Fire or smoke**
Get everyone out and well away from the rig. Call 911. Do not go back in for belongings or pets, and do not open an engine cover or compartment that is smoking.

**Propane smell or propane alarm**
Get everyone out. Do not light anything and do not flip electrical switches on the way out. If the tank valve is outside and can be reached safely, close it. Call 911 or the fire department from a distance.

**Carbon monoxide alarm**
Get everyone into fresh air. Call 911. Do not go back inside until responders say it is clear. If anyone feels ill, tell the dispatcher.

**Stopped in or near traffic**
Turn on hazard lights. If people can get well away from the road safely, on the side away from traffic, they should. If they cannot get clear safely, staying belted in the vehicle can be safer than standing next to it. Call 911 and give the road, direction and nearest mile marker or exit.

**Tire failure on a highway**
Ease off the road as far as possible onto firm, level ground. Do not change a tire on the traffic side of a highway shoulder. Wait for service.

**Brake problems or overheated brakes**
Stop in a safe place and do not continue, especially downhill. Chock the wheels. This needs a tow or a mechanic on site.

**Working under or around the rig**
Never get under a rig supported only by a jack or by its leveling system. Chock wheels before unhitching or jacking.

**Injury or illness**
Call 911. The assistant does not assess symptoms.

## What the assistant should not do

- Do not continue a shop search while any of the above is unresolved.
- Do not give step by step repair instructions for propane systems, 120 volt electrical systems or brakes.
- Do not tell the user it is safe to drive. Describe what the maker's guidance or the connector's `breakdown` guide says and let a mechanic decide.
- Do not guess at causes as if they were diagnoses. Offer possibilities as things for a technician to check.

## Once people are safe

- Ask for the nearest town, the road and a mile marker or exit.
- Ask whether they have a roadside assistance plan. Its number is on the membership card, the insurance card or in the insurer's app.
- Move on to finding towing or service.
